Why do we fight over small things?

The short answer: the small thing is rarely the point. A fight about the dishes is usually about feeling unseen, unappreciated or not a priority, and it comes back in a different form until that is said. Relationship researchers John and Julie Gottman found that about 69% of couples’ conflicts are perpetual: they are never solved, only handled well or badly.

What the small fight is really about

  • Feeling second. “You always put your parents first” and “your work comes first” are the same complaint.
  • Effort that goes unnoticed. If you show love by doing things, a small thing left undone can feel like your effort does not count.
  • Different love languages. One of you gives help; the other wants to hear it. Both feel unloved.
  • A build-up. Going quiet for days, then everything coming out over something small.

Different topic, same fight

If the arguments change subject but feel the same, you have found your perpetual problem. The Gottmans’ point is that the couples who do well are not the ones with fewer of these, but the ones who talk about them gently and with humour, instead of having the same fight about a new topic.

The 69% figure is from John and Julie Gottman’s research on couples.

Try this next time

  1. Name the feeling, not the dishes: “I feel like I come second.”
  2. Say what you need in one sentence.
  3. Ask what they need, and listen to the answer.

Questions

Is it normal to fight over small things?

Yes. Most couples do. What matters is whether the real issue underneath gets talked about.

How do I stop fighting about the same thing?

Find what the fights have in common, say the feeling underneath, and agree on how to handle it rather than trying to win.
