How do I get my Instagram data?

  1. In the Instagram app, open Settings → Accounts Centre.
  2. Go to Your information and permissions → Export / Download your information.
  3. Choose Create Export → Choose your Instagram account.
  4. Set Date Range to All time for the best results.
  5. Set Format to JSON. PsycheAI cannot read the HTML format.
  6. Set Media Quality to lower quality to save space on your device. Start export.
  7. Instagram emails you a download link, usually within a few hours. Download it to your device and upload it below.

How PsycheAI works

🔒

Your data stays with you

Your .zip is opened and read inside this browser tab. The archive itself is never uploaded — not to us, not to anyone.

Never leaves this device

  • The .zip file, and everything in it that the summary does not name
  • Every video, and every photo beyond the handful you agree to send
  • The other side of every DM — counted, then thrown away unread
  • Your full written report, which is saved only in this browser

Sent to be read

  • A bounded summary: your counts, your timings, and a sample of your own words
  • Up to 14 of your own photos, shrunk small — one switch sends none at all
  • A short version of your profile, when you hand someone your code

Where it actually goes

Your archive is unzipped and boiled down to that summary here, on your machine, before anything is sent. The summary — never the archive — is then posted to the PsycheAI server, which does exactly one thing with it: hands it to the language model that writes your profile (Google Gemini or Anthropic Claude), and passes the answer back to you.

The server has to sit in the middle because an API key cannot be shipped inside a web page — anyone who opened the page could read the key and spend it. So it relays, and that is all it does. Your summary exists there only for the seconds your request is in flight. It is never written to disk, never put in a database, and never logged.

🛡️

Could anyone else get at it?

There is no account, no database, and no stored copy of your data to steal.

There is nothing to breach. Almost every leak you have read about is a database being copied. PsycheAI does not have one. There is no sign-up, no password, no email address, no user table, no uploads folder. The server writes nothing to disk and keeps nothing once a request finishes, so there is no pile of anyone's data sitting somewhere waiting to be taken — including yours.

Your report is yours alone. It is saved in this browser's own storage, on this device, locked to this site. No other website can read it. The server never receives it — it is assembled here from the model's answer and stays here. It remains until you press Delete everything or clear your browser data.

Your QR code is not a lookup. It does not point at a record on a server: the short version of your profile is packed inside the code itself. There is no account behind it, and nothing to look up — so there is nothing there to hack either.

What this page will not promise you

Two things sit outside this app, and it would be dishonest to leave them out. The summary is read by Google or Anthropic, whichever model this server is set up with, and what they do with it is covered by their API terms rather than by anything here. And your report is only as private as the device it is on — anyone holding your unlocked phone can read it, exactly as they could read any site you are signed into.

If you would rather hold the whole chain yourself, you can: PsycheAI is open source and runs on your own machine with your own API key, in which case the "server" in all of the above is simply your own computer.

Your code, and matching

No account, no database, nothing to look up.

Your profile is squeezed down to a handful of short phrases and packed inside the QR code itself. That is the whole thing — the code is not a link to a profile, it is the profile. Your long report is not in there and never travels with it.

When someone scans you, they first choose what they are asking: romantic, family / friends, or professional. Those are genuinely different questions — you can be a great colleague and a poor housemate — so the report answers the one that was picked, in full, rather than three at once. Pick professional and it asks one more: whether you are colleagues, whether you manage them, or whether you report to them. Managing someone and working for them are not the same problem, and advice about delegation is useless to somebody with nobody to delegate to. It comes back with a score, five scored dimensions chosen for what you asked, an honest verdict, what works, what will rub, and specific advice for each of you about the other. Scan again to ask a different question.

Sharing your code is a choice you make one person at a time. Anyone you give it to keeps what is in it, so treat it the way you would a photo of yourself.

⚖️

The honest bit

This is a mirror and a conversation starter. It is not a test.

Not a psychometric instrument, not a diagnosis, not a background check. It is a language model reading traces of your behaviour, and it is often wrong — which is why it tells you how sure it is. A low match score is a list of things worth talking about, not a reason to walk away. A high one is not a promise.

What it is told never to do

  • Say anything about other people in your data or your photos — they never agreed to this
  • Guess at your sexuality, health, immigration status or politics unless you said it yourself
  • Judge anyone by their appearance, or by who they follow
  • Treat a handful of photos as proof — Instagram is where people post their best day of the month
