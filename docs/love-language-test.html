The five love languages

The love languages are five ways people tend to give and receive care: words of affirmation, quality time, acts of service, gifts and physical touch. Most people lean on one or two, and the way someone likes to receive care is often different from the way they give it.

How your data shows them

  • Words of affirmation show in warm comments, thank-yous and the encouragement you write to friends.
  • Quality time shows in plans you make, meet-ups you post and long conversations you keep going.
  • Acts of service show in the favours, help and logistics you take on in your messages.
  • Gifts show in what you mark and celebrate: birthdays, surprises, things you buy for people.
  • Physical touch is hardest to see in data, so it is named only when the evidence is clear.

Receiving and giving, separately

Your Psyche Card shows how you tend to show care, strongest first. The Psyche Report adds how you like to receive it, and explains each with the moments in your own data that point to it.

Using it with friends and family

Love languages are most useful between two people. Send a friend or family member your Psyche Card link and Psyche Sync compares how each of you shows care, with simple things each of you could do for the other. It is free, and never about romance.

Questions

Is this the official love languages test?

No. The five love languages come from Gary Chapman’s book; PsycheAI is not connected to it. It gives a reading inferred from your behaviour, not a questionnaire score.

Do I need to answer any questions?

No. Your love languages are read from your own Instagram data, alongside your MBTI, Big Five and values.
