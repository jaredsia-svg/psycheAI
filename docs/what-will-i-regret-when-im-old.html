What will I regret most when I’m old?

The short answer: people near the end of life rarely regret what they did; they regret what they put off. Bronnie Ware, a palliative carer, heard the same five again and again: not living true to themselves, working too much, not saying how they felt, losing touch with friends, and not letting themselves be happier. Which one applies to you shows in how you spend your time now.

The five most common regrets

  1. Not living a life true to myself

    Living the life others expected instead of the one they wanted.

  2. Working too hard

    Missing time with family and friends for work.

  3. Not expressing my feelings

    Keeping the peace by keeping quiet.

  4. Losing touch with friends

    Letting friendships fade in the busy years.

  5. Not letting myself be happier

    Staying in old habits out of fear of change.

From Bronnie Ware’s The Top Five Regrets of the Dying (2012), based on her years as a palliative carer: observations, not a scientific survey.
